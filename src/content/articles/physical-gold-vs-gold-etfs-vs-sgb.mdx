## 1. Deconstructing Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

When evaluating gold as an asset class, Indian households frequently overlook the enormous frictional friction embedded in physical precious metals. A true quantitative comparison requires evaluating the **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)** equation over an 8-year holding cycle:

$$
TCO = C_{entry} + \sum_{t=1}^{n} C_{holding} + C_{exit} + T_{statutory} - I_{coupon}
$$

Where:
- $C_{entry}$: Upfront purchase costs (GST, dealer premium, making charges).
- $C_{holding}$: Annual recurring drag (bank locker rental, insurance, ETF expense ratio).
- $C_{exit}$: Resale friction (melting deductions, dealer spreads, brokerage).
- $T_{statutory}$: Capital gains tax paid upon realization.
- $I_{coupon}$: Cash interest received during the holding period (applicable only to SGB).

## 3. The Takeaway for Portfolio Allocators

The numbers are unequivocal:
1. **Physical Jewelry is a consumption item**, not an investment. The upfront loss of $16\%$ to $20\%$ between making charges, GST, and melting loss permanently handicaps the compounding engine.
2. **Gold ETFs are optimal for tactical trading**, rebalancing, and liquidity needs under 3 years.
3. **SGB is mathematically superior** for any long-term investor with a 5 to 8-year horizon, producing **₹3,32,800 more net wealth** on a ₹5 Lakh allocation than physical jewelry.
